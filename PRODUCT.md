# Product

## Platform

web

## Stack

Astro, deployed to Cloudflare Pages.

## Users

The portfolio represents Nathan Pannell. Its primary audiences are:

- Recruiters and hiring teams evaluating Nathan for software engineering roles.
- Students looking for mentoring or practical guidance.
- Software professionals and builders interested in networking or collaboration.

The desired next step for each audience is to contact Nathan by email or through one of his social profiles.

## Product Purpose

Present Nathan as a software engineer and founder-style product builder who makes apps that bring people joy. The portfolio should make his current work easy to understand, lead with Parkdex, preserve and publish his existing writing, and turn interest into genuine conversations.

Success means qualified visitors understand what Nathan builds and why, then reach out with job opportunities, mentoring questions, or professional connections.

## Positioning

Nathan builds with the initiative and end-to-end ownership of a startup founder, but chooses products for the happiness, curiosity, and real-world experiences they create rather than optimizing primarily for revenue. Parkdex, a completionist guide to Vancouver Island parks, is the clearest current expression of that position.

## Operating Context

Visitors may arrive from LinkedIn, GitHub, X, a shared project, or a job application. They need to move quickly between a concise introduction, Parkdex, selected past work, writing, and direct contact routes.

The selected article and project-writing backlog has been migrated from the earlier site and private `NathanPannell/chiri-blog` repository. The new portfolio is deployed to Cloudflare Pages, but the custom domain still serves the older Vercel site until an explicit cutover. The most recent redesign may remain on staging until Nathan explicitly promotes it.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Build the site with Astro and deploy it on Cloudflare Pages.
- Make Parkdex the primary current project while retaining selected past projects as supporting evidence.
- Preserve the migrated blog posts and project writing while keeping future additions easy to author in Markdown or MDX.
- Provide direct calls to action for email, LinkedIn, GitHub, X, and other confirmed social profiles where appropriate.
- Keep public claims grounded in real projects, experience, posts, and source material. Do not invent customers, testimonials, metrics, or outcomes.
- The confirmed information architecture, project selection, and migration decisions are recorded below.

## Brand Commitments

- Use the name Nathan Pannell.
- Preserve the core identity expressed consistently across current profiles: “building products that make life more fun.”
- The voice should be positive, candid, curious, and technically credible. It should sound like Nathan's direct writing, not polished corporate positioning.
- Vancouver Island, Victoria, parks, hiking, camping, and time outdoors are genuine parts of Nathan's life and work.
- An outdoors-influenced direction is welcome, but it must not read as hippie, stoner, or generic wellness branding.
- The portfolio should convey ambition without glorifying grind culture, revenue maximization, or status for its own sake.

## Product Principles

1. Let shipped work prove the identity. Lead with Parkdex and concrete artifacts rather than résumé-style claims.
2. Make joy and curiosity legible. Show why a product makes life better or more fun, not only how it was engineered.
3. Be personal without becoming performative. Keep the voice warm, direct, and recognizably Nathan's.
4. Root the story in place. Vancouver Island is lived context for the work, not decorative outdoors branding.
5. Make reaching out feel natural. Every important journey should end with a clear, low-friction way to start a conversation.
